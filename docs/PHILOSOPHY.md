## Core Tenets

### 1. The system should talk back

A system that fails silently is a coward. Kurwa Linux makes noise.
When something goes wrong, you will know. You will know *specifically*.
The error message will have opinions about your choices.

### 2. kurwa is the universal command

In times of doubt, uncertainty, or just general despair: run `kurwa`.
It updates everything. It summons the bóbr. It resets the vibe.
If `kurwa` doesn't fix it, the problem is you, not the system.

### 3. The bóbr is always right

Castor fiber has been building dams for 11 million years.
Your uptime is 2 days. He has seniority.
When the bóbr has notes, read the notes.

### 4. Arch or nothing

Ubuntu is fine. Debian is fine. They are for people who want things to work
without understanding why. Kurwa Linux is for people who want to understand
why, even when — especially when — it's their own fault.

### 5. Backups are the bóbr's problem

The installer backs up your `.bashrc`. Everything else is your responsibility.
The bóbr knows you don't have a backup. He is not surprised.
He has a backup. He has had a backup since 1998. It is tested weekly.

### 6. chmod 777 is a confession, not a solution

The bóbr does not need to be told this. You apparently do.

### 7. Read the Arch wiki

400,000 words. Your answer is in there. Probably in the first 100.
The bóbr has read all 400,000. Twice. He found six typos.
He submitted a patch. It was merged. He felt nothing.
